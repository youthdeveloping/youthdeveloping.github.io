CASE 1 · THE TRAFFIC CRISIS

The Traffic Crisis

Traffic in the downtown area is terrible. Citizens are angry because it takes two hours to drive to work each day, causing stress, lateness, and frustration. Businesses are complaining, and pollution is increasing.

The city only has enough budget to choose one solution, so its decision could change the city's future.

A

Widen the Roads

Build bigger roads so more cars can travel through the city.

Look More 💧
OPTION A

Widen the Roads

Negative consequences:

  • A popular local park would need to be destroyed to make space for the new roads.
  • Families would lose a place to relax, children would have nowhere nearby to play, and sports teams and community groups would lose an important meeting area.
  • Hundreds of trees and plants would be removed, reducing green space and harming local wildlife.
  • The city may become noisier and more polluted because more people would be encouraged to drive through the area.
B

Build a Subway System

Build a new subway system so people can travel without using cars.

Look More 💧
OPTION B

Build a Subway System

Negative consequences:

  • The subway would use all of the city's money, leaving no budget for schools this year.
  • Schools might have to cancel field trips, sports programs, and extracurricular activities. They may also be unable to buy new books, computers, or classroom supplies.
  • Some schools could have to postpone repairs, resulting in outdated equipment and overcrowded classrooms.
  • Teachers, students, and parents may become frustrated because education would suffer while the city focuses all of its money on the subway project.
C

Ban Cars Completely

Ban cars from downtown so people must walk, cycle, or use public transport.

Look More 💧
OPTION C

Ban Cars Completely

Negative consequences:

  • Many businesses could lose customers because people may avoid shopping or eating downtown if they cannot drive there easily.
  • Some shops and restaurants might earn less money, reduce staff hours, or even close.
  • Elderly people, families with young children, and people with disabilities may find it difficult to walk or cycle long distances.
  • Many citizens would be angry because they would feel their freedom and convenience had been taken away, leading to complaints and protests against the new policy.
